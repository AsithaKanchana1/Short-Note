# 150 Q\&A

## Questions on Requirements Engineering

Below is a comprehensive set of 150 questions and answers covering the key topics in Requirements Engineering. These are suitable for academic study, exam preparation, or self-assessment. The questions span definitions, processes, models, techniques, and practical considerations

---

### **1. Introduction to Requirements Engineering**

1. **What is Requirements Engineering (RE)?**
RE is a set of activities concerned with identifying and communicating the purpose of a software-intensive system and the contexts in which it will be used[^4][^6].
2. **Why is Requirements Engineering important in software development?**
It acts as a bridge between real-world needs and technical solutions, ensuring the system delivers value to stakeholders[^4].
3. **List the main activities in Requirements Engineering.**
Elicitation, analysis, specification, validation, and management[^6][^7].
4. **What is the difference between a requirement and a specification?**
A requirement is a statement of need or constraint; a specification is a detailed, precise description of those requirements[^4][^6].
5. **What is the main goal of Requirements Engineering?**
To ensure the developed system meets stakeholders' needs and operates within defined constraints[^4][^6].

---

### **2. Types of Requirements**

6. **What are functional requirements?**
Statements of services the system should provide[^6].
7. **What are non-functional requirements?**
Constraints on the system such as performance, security, or usability[^6].
8. **Give an example of a functional requirement.**
"The system shall allow users to log in using a username and password."
9. **Give an example of a non-functional requirement.**
"The system shall respond to user input within 2 seconds."
10. **What is a domain requirement?**
Requirements derived from the domain of the application, such as industry-specific standards[^6].

---

### **3. Requirements Elicitation**

11. **What is requirements elicitation?**
The process of discovering requirements from stakeholders and other sources[^6][^7].
12. **Name three techniques for requirements elicitation.**
Interviews, questionnaires, and workshops[^6][^7].
13. **Who are stakeholders?**
People or organizations affected by or with an interest in the system[^6][^7].
14. **Why is stakeholder identification important?**
To ensure all relevant needs are considered and addressed[^4][^6].
15. **What is a common problem in requirements elicitation?**
Stakeholders may not know what they really want[^6].

---

### **4. Requirements Analysis**

16. **What is requirements analysis?**
The process of examining and refining requirements to resolve conflicts and clarify ambiguities[^6][^7].
17. **Why do requirements often conflict?**
Different stakeholders may have competing interests or priorities[^6][^7].
18. **What is requirements prioritization?**
Determining the relative importance of each requirement[^7].
19. **What is requirements negotiation?**
Resolving conflicts and agreeing on a set of requirements acceptable to all parties[^7].
20. **List two techniques for analyzing requirements.**
Use case modeling and data flow diagrams[^3][^6].

---

### **5. Requirements Specification**

21. **What is a requirements specification?**
A document that clearly and precisely describes the essential requirements of the system[^4][^6].
22. **What is the purpose of a requirements specification document?**
To provide a reference for system design, implementation, and validation[^4][^6].
23. **Name two common templates for requirements specification.**
IEEE 830 and Volere Shell[^4].
24. **What qualities should a good requirement have?**
Clear, unambiguous, complete, consistent, and testable[^6][^7].
25. **Why is traceability important in requirements specification?**
To track each requirement through design, implementation, and testing[^6].

---

### **6. Requirements Validation**

26. **What is requirements validation?**
Ensuring that requirements accurately reflect stakeholders' needs and are feasible[^4][^6][^7].
27. **What is the difference between validation and verification?**
Validation: building the right product; Verification: building the product right[^4].
28. **Name two techniques for requirements validation.**
Reviews and prototyping[^4][^6].
29. **What is a requirements review?**
A formal inspection of the requirements document to find errors or omissions[^6].
30. **Why is requirements validation important?**
To prevent costly errors later in development[^4][^6].

---

### **7. Requirements Management**

31. **What is requirements management?**
The process of handling changes to requirements throughout the project lifecycle[^6][^7].
32. **Why do requirements change?**
Evolving stakeholder needs, market changes, or technical discoveries[^6].
33. **What is a requirements baseline?**
A formally agreed set of requirements that can only be changed through a defined process[^6].
34. **What is impact analysis?**
Assessing the effects of a proposed change to requirements[^6].
35. **Why is version control important in requirements management?**
To track changes and maintain consistency[^6].

### **8. Modeling Requirements**

36. **What is requirements modeling?**
Creating abstract representations of requirements using diagrams or models[^3].
37. **Name three types of requirements models.**
Information structure models, dynamic models, scenario models[^3].
38. **What is a use case diagram?**
A graphical representation of system functionality from a user's perspective[^3][^6].
39. **What is a sequence diagram?**
A diagram showing interactions between actors and the system over time[^3].
40. **What is state-oriented modeling?**
Modeling system behavior in terms of states and transitions[^3].

### **9. Scenario Modeling**

41. **What is scenario modeling?**
Describing possible sequences of interactions between users and the system[^3].
42. **What is the purpose of scenario modeling in requirements engineering?**
To clarify requirements and anticipate system behavior in real-world situations[^3].
43. **Name two types of scenario diagrams.**
Sequence diagrams and communication diagrams[^3].
44. **What is a simple scenario?**
A basic sequence of interactions representing typical system use[^3].
45. **What is an advanced scenario?**
A more complex interaction, possibly involving exceptions or alternate flows[^3].

46. **What does it mean for a requirement to be unambiguous?**
It can only be interpreted in one way[^6][^7].
47. **What is requirement completeness?**
All necessary requirements are included[^6][^7].
48. **What is requirement consistency?**
No conflicting requirements exist[^6][^7].
